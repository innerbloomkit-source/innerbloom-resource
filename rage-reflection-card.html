Just For You

Nothing here is saved. Write freely.

01
Think about the last time it arrived. What was happening right before it — or did it come from nowhere?
Irritability and rage are among the most commonly reported experiences in perimenopause — some research suggests up to 70% of women describe them. They are linked to estrogen and progesterone fluctuation affecting how the brain regulates emotion. Not personality. Not weakness. A hormone pattern.
02
What does the hour after look like? What do you do with how that felt?
The shame that follows is almost universal. When a reaction arrives before its cause makes any sense, the mind fills the gap with self-blame. So many women describe apologising for a feeling they did not choose and could not explain.
03
Think about who you were a few years ago. What would she make of who you feel like right now?
Feeling like a stranger in your own life is one of the most reported experiences of this stage. Hormonal shifts can move your emotional baseline in ways that feel foreign — which is exactly why so many women say "this is not me."
04
If you could say one thing to the people around you — your partner, your kids, your colleagues — what would it be? The thing you have not found the words for yet.
Strain on the people closest to you is one of the most common things women carry — not because something is wrong with you, but because they do not understand what is happening either. Putting it into words is often the first relief.
05
What would it mean to you to finally understand why this is happening?
Women consistently say the same thing helped most — not a cure, but an explanation. Understanding that a feeling follows a hormone pattern, something that can be seen and tracked, is what turns fear back into control.
